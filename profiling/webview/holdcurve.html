The one-line answer

The hold cap is a real dial, and the two markets want opposite things. US is mean-reverting: Sharpe peaks at a ~20-day cap (0.993) and decays as you let winners run (0.688 at ∞). India is trend-persistent: Sharpe keeps climbing to a ~40-day plateau (1.238) and raw return maxes at ∞ (100.9%). Benchmarks 5yr: US +79.3%, India +50.2%.

Repeat-trade churn — how often the same stock gets re-entered

The same name gets bought, sold, and bought again constantly — and the time cap is the single biggest driver. A tight 5-day cap kicks winners out early, so momentum leaders keep re-qualifying: US churns each unique name 5.3× and 93% of all trades are re-entries. Loosen the cap and churn falls monotonically toward ~2.0× / ~70% at ∞ — you hold the same winners instead of re-buying them. Reading the result

  • US = cut winners early. Sharpe rises 5→20 then falls monotonically; drawdown worsens the longer you hold (-12.0% at 20 → -16.6% at ∞). A tight ~20-day time-stop is doing risk control the exit rules don't.
  • India = let winners run. Return climbs almost the whole way to ∞ (+63.5% at 20 → +100.9% at ∞) while drawdown barely moves; the 30–40 band is the risk-adjusted sweet spot, ∞ the return-max.
  • The knee is sharp, not flat. Both markets gain most of their Sharpe going from a 5-day to a 20–40-day cap; sub-10-day caps churn (US 1168, India 1327 trades) and bleed to costs without improving risk-adjusted return.
  • Strategy-specific: the momentum-quant US strats (mq_us1/us2/us3) all Sharpe-peak at hold=20 regardless of market; mean-reversion-flavored names tolerate longer holds. mq_in2 is the one weak sheep (Sharpe <0.4 in US) — hold tuning can't fix a broken signal.
